# The sales play

Numbered so the scorer and meeting-prep can cite plays by number. Write what your best rep
actually does, from transcripts, not what a sales book says.

## The narrative arc (first and second calls)

1. **Credibility drop.** <!-- who we are, in one line, with the one fact that earns attention -->
2. **Origin story.** <!-- why we built this -->
3. **The reframe.** <!-- the options they think they have, and the one they did not know about -->
4. **Name their pain before they do.**

## Discovery

5. **Ask before pitching.**
6. **Mine the acute, quantified pain.** "What does that cost you a month?"
7. **Wish list / hate list.**

## Qualification (do not skip)

8. **Budget reality.** "How do projects like this get funded here?"
9. **Decision process.** "Who else weighs in, and in what order?"
10. **Compelling event.** "What happens if nothing changes by <date>?"

## Demo

11. **Demo to the pain they named,** three screens, then stop and ask.

## The close

12. **Engineer the next step** in the room: date, attendees, what each side brings.
13. **Mutual action.** They commit to something too (data, an intro, a review).

## Buying signals (same-day tier)

A call where the prospect does one of these is tiered `same_day` by `sales-intake`. Edit to
what your buyers actually say.

- Asks for a trial or access.
- "Can my whole team see this?"
- Asks about pricing, contract terms or procurement.

## Anti-patterns (coaching flags)

- Dodging an integration or capability question instead of answering it truthfully.
- Feature dumping before the pain is named.
- "We'll circle back" as a next step.
